How to Turn a YouTube Tutorial Into Step-by-Step Instructions

YouTube is where the best tutorials live, and where the steps are buried deepest. Here's how to pull them out of a long video and onto a list you can actually follow.

CG
Chantel Gia, Founder & CEO of InspiraDo
November 20, 2026 · 5 min read

Quick answer

To turn a YouTube tutorial into step-by-step instructions:

  1. Open the description and chapters first, where creators often list supplies and timestamps.
  2. Watch once at 1.5–2× speed to learn the shape of the project.
  3. Write one action per step, pulling from what's said, what's shown, and the description.
  4. List materials and tools separately, with the amounts the creator says or holds up.
  5. Keep the link, and the timestamp of any tricky part, so you can jump back and credit the creator.

Or let an app do it: paste the YouTube link into InspiraDo and tap ✨ Generate project with AI. Because it watches the video, you get the most complete steps, materials and tools of any platform, in under a minute.

The tutorials I most wanted to make were always the long ones: the eighteen-minute builds, the full recipes, the "I flipped this thrift-store dresser" videos. And those were exactly the ones I never finished, because following along meant propping my phone up, scrubbing back to catch a measurement, losing my place, and sitting through an ad with paint drying on my hands. That's the gap this guide is about.

Why YouTube tutorials are hard to follow

YouTube is where creators go deep, which is the best and the worst thing about it. Every step is in there. It's just spread across a long video:

  • The supplies are read aloud once, usually near the start, and never repeated.
  • The one tip that saves the project shows up around minute 11.
  • A step can be a quick cut or a slow demonstration, so "pause at every cut" doesn't map as neatly as it does on a 30-second video.
  • Ads interrupt right when you're trying to follow along.
  • Your hands aren't free. You're constantly reaching over to scrub back and find your place.

So following a YouTube tutorial really means pulling those scattered steps into one ordered list you can work from without touching the video again.

Method 1: Write the steps out yourself

Free, works for any video, and takes about 15–20 minutes for a longer tutorial.

  1. Start with the description and chapters. Good creators list supplies, links and timestamps in the description. The chapter markers under the progress bar are a free outline, so use them as your rough steps.
  2. Watch once at 1.5–2× speed. Tap the gear icon, then Playback speed. You're just learning the shape: what you're making and the main stages.
  3. Go back and capture the details. Jump to each chapter, pause, and write each step as a single action that starts with a verb: "Sand the top," not "then the sanding part."
  4. Catch the supplies as they're said or shown. Write the quantity and size the moment the creator says it or holds it up to the camera. They rarely repeat it.
  5. Split materials from tools. Materials get used up (paint, wood, glue). Tools you keep (a drill, a sander). It makes shopping much easier later. There's a full guide in how to create a materials list from a DIY video.
  6. Note the tips and warnings. "Let the first coat dry fully," "pilot the hole or the bit will wander." These are the details people skip and regret.
  7. Keep the link and the key timestamps. So you can jump straight back to the tricky part, and credit the creator.
Tip: paste the timestamp next to any step you know you'll rewatch, like Step 6 — miter cut (8:12). On a long video, that one habit saves the most time.

Why YouTube is the best source for this

A short video gives an app only the narration and the caption to read. YouTube gives it the whole video, so InspiraDo can watch the demonstration: the tool that's used but never named, the amount that's shown but never said, the note in the corner of the screen. That's why YouTube tutorials come out the most complete, and why the how-to or recipe you've been avoiding because it's "too long" is often the easiest kind to turn into a project.

Combining several videos into one plan

Bigger projects send you to three different creators for three parts of the job. Instead of juggling tabs, open a project, tap Add a video to attach the others, then tap ✨ Combine into one plan. InspiraDo merges them into a single set of steps without the repeats: one plan from a whole playlist's worth of research.

Give the creator their credit

Whatever method you use, keep the link and the creator's name with your steps. Like the video, subscribe, and if you make the thing, tell them in the comments. Long tutorials take real work to film, and that's how creators keep making the ones we all learn from.
