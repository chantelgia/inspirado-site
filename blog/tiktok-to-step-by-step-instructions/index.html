How to Turn TikTok Videos Into Step-by-Step Instructions

TikTok is the best place to find a project and the hardest place to follow one. Here's how to get the steps out of a 60-second video and onto a list you can actually work from.

CG
Chantel Gia, Founder & CEO of InspiraDo
November 20, 2026 · 5 min read

Quick answer

To turn a TikTok into step-by-step instructions:

  1. Save the video and watch it once all the way through.
  2. Collect the steps from everywhere they hide: the voiceover, the text on screen, the caption and the comments.
  3. Write one action per step, starting each with a verb.
  4. List materials and tools separately.
  5. Keep a link to the original so you can rewatch it and credit the creator.

Or let an app do it: share the TikTok to InspiraDo, tap ✨ Generate project with AI, and you get numbered steps, materials and tools in about 30 seconds.

For years my saved folders were full of things I was definitely going to make. I almost never did, because every time I wanted to start, I had to dig the video back up, scrub through it, pause, rewind, and try to remember what the caption said. With glue on my hands. That's the gap this guide is about.

Why TikTok tutorials are so hard to follow

A TikTok tutorial is built to be watched, not followed. It's short, it's often sped up, and the instructions are spread across four or five places at once:

  • The voiceover. Most of the real instructions are spoken, quickly.
  • Text on screen. Measurements, product names and "let it dry overnight" often appear for a second and vanish.
  • The caption. Some creators list the supplies there. Some don't.
  • The comments. Answers to "what paint is that?" usually live in the creator's replies or a pinned comment.
  • Part 2. Longer projects get split across several videos.

So "turning a TikTok into instructions" really means gathering those pieces into one place and putting them in order.

Method 1: Write the steps out yourself

Free, works for any video, and takes about 15 minutes for a typical tutorial.

  1. Watch it once without pausing. You're just learning the shape of the project: what you're making and roughly how many stages there are.
  2. Slow it down. On most versions of the app, pressing and holding the video brings up playback speed. Half speed makes fast hands much easier to follow. Turn on captions if the video has them.
  3. Pause at every cut. In most tutorials, a new shot means a new step. Write each one as a single action that starts with a verb: "Cut the arch shapes," not "and then the arches."
  4. Read the caption and the comments. Look for a supplies list, a pinned comment, answers from the creator, and links to part 2.
  5. Split materials from tools. Materials get used up (paint, glue, tape). Tools you keep (scissors, a glue gun). It makes shopping much easier later. There's a full guide on that in how to create a materials list from a DIY video.
  6. Add the tips and warnings. Drying times, "this gets hot," "use two coats." These are the details people skip and regret.
  7. Save the link with your notes. You'll want to rewatch the tricky parts, and the creator deserves the credit.
Tip: keep the same layout for every project: Project name, link, materials, tools, steps, notes. After a few projects you'll fill it in without thinking.

What about TikToks with no talking?

Some tutorials are just music and text on screen. There's no voiceover or caption for an AI to read from the link, so these are the hardest kind to convert.

The fix is screenshots. Pause on each frame where a step or measurement appears, take a screenshot, then in InspiraDo tap 📸 Add from screenshots and pick them. The AI reads the text in your screenshots and builds the project from those. If you're doing it by hand, the same screenshots make a quick visual checklist.

Combining several TikToks into one plan

For bigger projects you'll often watch three creators do the same thing three slightly different ways. Instead of switching between videos, open a project, tap Add a video to attach the others, then tap ✨ Combine into one plan. InspiraDo merges them into a single set of steps without the duplicates.

Give the creator their credit

Whatever method you use, keep the link and the creator's name with your steps. Follow them, and if you make the thing, tell them in the comments or tag them. Creators make the free tutorials we all learn from, and that's how they keep going.
